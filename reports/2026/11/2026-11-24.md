- **🔥 [LLMs Memorize Their Own Hallucinations During Post-Training](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26863)** (`0 upvotes`)
  - *Summary*: Reasoning domains have shaped the post-training recipes of frontier models even though they are trained and deployed in domains beyond math and code. In this paper, we study the consequences of directly adopting reasoning-centric training recipes, e.g., the use of outcome-based rewards for reinforcement learning (RL), injection of "cognitive" behaviors such as proposing and backtracking from hypotheses in reasoning chains, etc., for knowledge tasks. We hypothesize that these decisions, while useful for reasoning, inadvertently encourage models to memorize hallucinations they themselves generate in their long reasoning chains when trained on knowledge tasks. To study this, we train models with standard post-training, extract their self-generated hallucinations during RL, and track memorization throughout the rest of training. Our experiments on six open models across two model families and two architectures confirm our hypothesis. We find that models' recall of hallucinations reinforced during training increases by 4.83% on average, compared to just 0.17% for self-hallucinations that receive no training signal. In fact, models even memorize false facts that they immediately retract in their reasoning; a backtracking template inherited from reasoning-centric training that has no benefit for knowledge recall. Furthermore, we find that memorization is strongest on the hardest training questions, and rewarding a hallucination makes the model less likely to abstain from it later (by 7.23% on average). Overall, our work identifies and systematically studies a previously unexamined pathway through which models learn hallucinations during training.

  - *Summary*: Human demonstrations provide a rich source of bimanual dexterous skills, yet transferring them to robot hands remains challenging. Existing methods primarily optimize object trajectory tracking, often overlooking how human hands interact with the object. Even with accurate tracking, robots may contact incorrect object parts or slide their fingers, resulting in unstable manipulation. We present DexDuet, a framework that learns contact-preserving bimanual dexterity from a single human demonstration. DexDuet employs two interaction rewards to preserve human hand-object configurations and contacts. To enable coordinated manipulation, we factorize the bimanual policy into two weight-sharing per-hand policies that condition on each other's states. We further propose assisted reference-state initialization (A-RSI), an adaptive curriculum that adjusts object assistance and training starts across demonstration segments to overcome difficult manipulation phases. Across four benchmarks spanning three simulators and five robot embodiments, DexDuet improves object-tracking ADD-AUC by 11 to 41 points over the corresponding baselines while consistently achieving better contact preservation. Notably, among 122 paired runs with comparable object-tracking accuracy, DexDuet preserves the human's contacts better in 117 cases. Code and videos are available at https://rcl-robotics.github.io/Dexduet.io/
